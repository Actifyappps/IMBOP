# IMBOP Billing & Payments Blueprint

## Purpose

The IMBOP Billing & Payments capability turns the approved commercial model into an auditable institutional commercial layer.

The customer is the **Institution**.

Individual institution members are not billed for ordinary IMBOP participation.

The commercial unit is the institution's number of **IMBOP Onboards**.

## Product-facing experience

The institution should eventually be able to:

**SEE BILL → VIEW INVOICE → PAY → CONFIRM PAYMENT → RECEIVE RECEIPT → VIEW PAYMENT HISTORY**

Supported payment channels are designed to include:

- **M-Pesa**
- **Airtel Money**
- **Bank Transfer**
- **Card**
- **Other approved channels**

The payment rail moves money. IMBOP remains the application's source of truth for the billing state.

## Commercial rule

**0–50 IMBOP Onboards: FREE**

Beyond 50, the institution moves to the applicable numbers-based plan.

The first transition month may use the approved discounted minimum of **KES 5,000**.

The KES 5,000 transition amount is the **total discounted transition-month invoice**, not an additional fee on top of the normal plan charge.

## Billing architecture

The billing domain is separated into:

1. **Pricing Plans** — what SE says a plan costs.
2. **Institution Billing Account** — the institution's current commercial state.
3. **Invoices** — what is owed for a defined billing period.
4. **Invoice Lines** — why the invoice total exists.
5. **Payment Intents** — a requested payment journey before provider settlement.
6. **Payments** — money received/confirmed.
7. **Payment Allocations** — which invoice a payment settles.
8. **Receipts** — evidence that a confirmed payment was received.
9. **Billing Events** — auditable operational history.
10. **Communications** — notifications about billing events.

Canonical flow:

**ONBOARD COUNT → PLAN RESOLUTION → INVOICE → PAYMENT INTENT → PAYMENT → ALLOCATION → RECEIPT → RECONCILIATION → COMMUNICATION → AUDIT**

## Important separation

Pricing is not billing.

- Pricing defines the commercial rules.
- Billing calculates and records obligations.
- A payment intent represents an intended payment journey.
- Payments record settlement.
- Allocations connect settlement to invoices.
- Receipts evidence confirmed settlement.
- Communications tells people what happened.

No billing screen should silently change pricing policy.

## Invoice lifecycle

**DRAFT → ISSUED → PARTIALLY PAID → PAID**

Exception states:

- **OVERDUE**
- **VOID**
- **WAIVED**

Free/founding invoices may be recorded as **WAIVED** rather than pretending that a KES 0 invoice was paid.

## Payment lifecycle

A future customer payment flow is designed around:

**CREATED → PENDING → SUCCEEDED / FAILED / CANCELLED / EXPIRED**

Provider-specific details belong in the payment-intent/provider boundary, not in the core invoice model.

## Receipt rule

A confirmed payment should have an auditable receipt.

Receipt numbering is generated by IMBOP, for example:

**IMBOP-RCP-2026-000001**

The receipt records:

- institution
- payment
- receipt number
- amount
- currency
- issue time
- recipient information where available
- notes/metadata

A confirmed payment automatically creates a receipt record through the internal database trigger. The platform can also issue/retrieve a receipt through the controlled receipt RPC.

## Payment model

The internal billing foundation is **provider-neutral**.

It supports payment-method concepts for:

- manual
- bank transfer
- M-Pesa
- Airtel Money
- card
- other

The provider integration itself is deliberately separated from the accounting model.

When payment rails are connected, the expected pattern is:

**IMBOP PAY NOW → PAYMENT PROVIDER → CALLBACK/CONFIRMATION → IMBOP PAYMENT → ALLOCATION → RECEIPT**

No provider should become the source of truth for the commercial state.

## Security model

Institution billing data is institution-scoped.

Institution administrators may read their institution's billing information.

SE platform administrators control:

- invoice creation
- payment recording
- payment-intent creation during controlled operations
- overdue state
- billing administration
- receipt issuance controls

Direct customer-side writes to financial records are not allowed.

Platform financial operations are exposed through tightly scoped RPCs that verify platform permission.

## Design principle

> **Money movement and billing state are related, but they are not the same thing.**

A payment provider can tell IMBOP that money moved.

IMBOP must still determine:

- who paid
- which institution paid
- which invoice was intended
- how much was allocated
- whether the invoice is settled
- whether there is an overpayment
- whether there is an outstanding balance
- what receipt was issued
- what communication should be issued
- what audit event was recorded
- who changed the record

## Acceptance principle

Before connecting real payment rails, the billing domain should be able to answer:

> **Who owes what, for which period, under which plan, what has been received, what has been allocated, what remains outstanding, what receipt was issued, and who changed the record?**

## Provider payment rails

The institution-facing payment flow now follows:

Outstanding Bill → Paying How Much Now → Payment Method → Payment Intent → Provider Prompt / Checkout → Provider Confirmation → Billing Payment → Invoice Allocation → Receipt

Supported customer channels:
- M-PESA
- Airtel Money
- Bank Transfer
- Card

### Partial payments

Institutions may pay less than the outstanding invoice balance. The requested amount is bound server-side to the selected invoice and cannot exceed its current outstanding balance. The invoice becomes PARTIALLY_PAID until fully allocated, then PAID.

### M-PESA

The imbop-payment-provider Edge Function provides the M-PESA adapter boundary. It:
1. authenticates the institution administrator;
2. creates the institution-scoped payment intent;
3. obtains a Daraja access token server-side;
4. requests the M-PESA STK prompt;
5. stores provider references and moves the intent to PENDING;
6. receives the provider callback;
7. verifies the callback against the payment intent and exact amount;
8. finalizes the payment idempotently;
9. allocates the payment to the invoice;
10. issues the IMBOP receipt.

The prompt is not the confirmation. The provider callback/verification is the confirmation.

Provider credentials remain server-side and are not committed to GitHub. The branch defaults to the Safaricom sandbox endpoint until production configuration is deliberately supplied. Safaricom describes Daraja 3.0 as the bridge for integrating M-PESA APIs into web and mobile applications.

### Airtel Money, Bank Transfer and Card

These channels use the same provider-neutral payment-intent and ledger architecture. Their provider adapters remain configuration-gated until the respective credentials, sandbox contracts and callback/reconciliation rules are supplied and tested.

### Reconciliation and idempotency

Provider references, checkout references and payment-intent IDs are persisted. Duplicate callbacks cannot create a second payment because provider finalization uses an idempotency key derived from the payment intent. Amount mismatches, expired intents and overpayments are rejected server-side.


## Reconciliation hardening

The M-PESA adapter now also supports an authenticated **status query** for a pending payment intent. It uses the stored CheckoutRequestID and the STK Push Query endpoint, so the institution can explicitly request a fresh provider status when a callback is delayed. A successful query is finalized only when the provider response supplies the amount and M-PESA receipt needed by the ledger; otherwise the intent remains PENDING. Terminal non-success results move the intent to FAILED.

The database also protects the ledger against concurrent allocation races: invoice allocations lock and validate the target invoice/payment, provider references are unique per provider, outstanding calculations count only confirmed payments, and stale payment intents can be expired by a service-only operation.
